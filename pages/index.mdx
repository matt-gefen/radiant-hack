# Introduction

Welcome to my home table ttrpg rules called **_Guardians_**. This game is a tabletop role-playing game (TTRPG), played with paper, pencils and dice.

## What is this game?

In **_Guardians_**, Players find themselves in the shattered world of **Myriad**: the beautiful and dangerous realm born from a cataclysm that merged the world of humans with the realm of spirits and demons. Players create their own **Guardian Spirits** and are tasked with protecting their community and rebuilding connections between people and places scattered across a landscape in flux.

They go on expeditions to accomplish quests, fight monsters, explore ruins, and gather power. During interludes between those journeys, they build, heal, and learn. Eventually they may end up changing or saving the world.

This is guided by a set of rules and a game master (GM) who narrates the story and oversees gameplay. Unlike a book or visual media, the power of a TTRPG is that we get to tell a story together that changes naturally in response to your decisions.

## How does it work?

Everything inside **_Guardians_** is collaborative.

You build your community and home together, you create your **Guardian Spirits** together, and you make the world evolve together.

Each session is made of scenes, like in a movie. Each scene plays out as a bunch of back-and-forth between the GM and the players. The GM describes what the scene is like, the players tell the GM what they do, and the GM makes the world react to those actions depending on the outcomes of certain dice rolls.

Each time you and your friends meet to play **_Guardians_** is a session which can last between 1 and 4 hours, or even more. The amount of time each session takes depends on a lot of factors.

Each group can play very differently. Some might take their time while others might rush into things head-on, but it also depends on everyone's availability.

During sessions, tension rises during scenes, and the players encounter narrative unknowns. To decide what happens during those moments, the group rolls dice because they want to be on the edge of their seats. They want to be surprised by where the story takes them next.
